# Literature Review: Repurposing Beyond the Nearest Analog (Idea 1)

Prepared for RECOMB 2027 submission planning. Scope: verify the central novelty claim (debiased-residual training, DebiasedDTA-style, applied to indication/repurposing prediction instead of binding affinity), check for prior work on chemical-similarity shortcuts in DTI/repurposing, confirm LINCS L1000 / PubMedBERT-style text-embedding usage, confirm biomedical-text leakage as a documented failure mode, and confirm that every planned dataset (LINCS L1000, ChEMBL indications, Repurposing Hub, UniProt) is an established public resource. All entries below were retrieved and read (abstract or full text passage) through literature search tools during this review; nothing was fabricated. Where a detail could not be confirmed, that is stated explicitly rather than guessed.

## 2. Dataset legitimacy confirmation (hard requirement)

Each dataset the proposal plans to use is an established, publicly available resource with a substantial independent citation record. For each, 2-3 confirmed citing/describing papers are listed below (titles/venues as retrieved; this is not exhaustive, only a legitimacy spot-check).

**LINCS L1000 (gene-expression signatures)** — confirmed standard:
- Subramanian et al., "A Next Generation Connectivity Map: L1000 Platform and the First 1,000,000 Profiles," *Cell* 171(6), 2017 (PMC5990023) — the foundational dataset paper.
- Duan et al. / Koleti et al.-style resource paper, "Systematic Quality Control Analysis of LINCS Data," *CPT: Pharmacometrics & Systems Pharmacology* 2016 (PMC5192966) — independent QC analysis of the dataset.
- Pham et al. (DeepCE), *Nat. Mach. Intell.* 2021, and Keenan et al./Clue.io "L2S2" search engine, *Nucleic Acids Research* 2025 (PMC12230732) — both are independent downstream uses, confirming the dataset remains actively used nearly a decade after release.

**ChEMBL (bioactivity / indications)** — confirmed standard:
- Gaulton et al., "ChEMBL: a large-scale bioactivity database for drug discovery," *Nucleic Acids Research* 2012 (PMC3245175) — founding paper.
- Gaulton et al., "The ChEMBL database in 2017," *Nucleic Acids Research* 2017 (PMC5210557), and Zdrazil et al., "The ChEMBL Database in 2023," *Nucleic Acids Research* 2024 (PMC10767899) — periodic update papers showing sustained maintenance and use.
- Mendez/Papadatos-lineage downstream work, e.g., Bioactivity-explorer (PMC6617623, 2019) and the dedicated "Drug and Clinical Candidate Drug Data in ChEMBL" paper (*J. Med. Chem.* 2025, PMC12516679), confirm ChEMBL specifically curates clinical/indication-relevant drug data, not only raw assay bioactivity.

**Drug Repurposing Hub (Broad Institute)** — confirmed standard:
- Corsello et al., "The Drug Repurposing Hub: a next-generation drug library and information resource," *Nat. Med.* 2017 (PMC5568558) — founding paper (the abstract text itself was not retrievable through the search tool, but the citation record and downstream use below confirm it is the well-known Broad resource).
- Independent screening papers that explicitly use the Hub's compound library/annotations: "High-Throughput Screening of the Repurposing Hub Library ... Candida" (*J. Fungi* 2023, PMC10532723) and "High-Throughput Screens of Repurposing Hub and DOS Chemical Libraries ... Schistosoma mansoni" (*Int. J. Mol. Sci.* 2025, PMC12154458) — both use the named, annotated 4,000-5,440-compound Hub library exactly as the proposal describes.

**UniProt (protein function text)** — confirmed standard:
- UniProt Consortium, "The Universal Protein Resource (UniProt): an expanding universe of protein information," *Nucleic Acids Research* 2006 (PMC1347523), and subsequent biennial updates, e.g., "UniProt: the Universal Protein Knowledgebase in 2023," *Nucleic Acids Research* 2023 (PMC9825514) — UniProt publishes a dedicated, heavily cited NAR database update roughly every 1-2 years, which is itself strong evidence of continuous public maintenance.
- Downstream use for exactly the proposed purpose (free-text function descriptions driving ML): STAR-GO (PMC13120692, 2025) and DeepGOZero (PMC9235501, *Bioinformatics* 2022) both use UniProt/GO function text/axioms for zero-shot protein function prediction, the same representational move the proposal plans to make for drug-target matching.

**Overall**: all four datasets are well-established, non-invented, and each has multiple independent, recent (2022-2025) papers using them in closely analogous ways. This should satisfy a RECOMB reviewer's concern about dataset legitimacy, provided the write-up cites the specific resource papers above rather than only the raw download.

## 3. The novelty gap: is DebiasedDTA-style debiased-residual training genuinely unapplied to indication/repurposing prediction?

**Short answer: yes, substantially, but not completely cleanly — one close prior-work paper needs explicit differentiation.**

1. **DebiasedDTA itself (papers #1-2 above)** is scoped entirely to *binding-affinity regression* (BDB, KIBA), with warm/cold-chemical/cold-protein/cold-both splits defined over protein-chemical pairs. It does not touch disease/indication labels at all. Its own "Future directions" paragraph explicitly flags "applying our method to non-DTA prediction tasks" as unexplored future work — i.e., the original authors themselves identify this as an open gap, which is a strong citable hook for novelty.

2. **TAPB (#3)** extends the "debias via a weak/confounder model" idea within DTI, but (a) still predicts binding/interaction, not disease indication, and (b) targets a different bias (protein/target popularity as a causal confounder) via interventional/backdoor-adjustment training rather than a DebiasedDTA-style guide-model importance-reweighting scheme. It is evidence that debiasing-for-generalization is an active sub-area in DTI, but not a scoop on this proposal's specific target (indication prediction) or specific mechanism (chemical-analog bias).

3. **The closest genuine prior work is "Adaptive debiasing learning for drug repositioning" (DRDM, #4).** It is the only paper found that combines the words "debiasing" and "drug repositioning" in a training-time reweighting framework. However, it targets a **different bias** (graph-degree/popularity bias in drug-disease association graphs — a few drugs/diseases dominate the training graph) rather than **chemical nearest-neighbor-analog bias** (the proposal's bias is defined over molecular similarity, measured independent of the graph, via an explicit zero-training NN-search model). The mechanisms also differ: DRDM reweights graph edges inside a GNN with contrastive learning; the proposal's plan (reweighting or product-of-experts against a frozen NN-search "guide") is structurally identical to DebiasedDTA's guide/predictor design, just retargeted. This paper must be cited and explicitly distinguished in the related-work section — reviewers familiar with the repositioning-debiasing literature will likely surface it.

4. **The "chemical similarity inflates apparent repurposing accuracy" observation** has real precedent outside the ML-debiasing literature: Guney 2017 (#7) and the broader scaffold-split-overestimation literature (#5, #6) establish that the *diagnosis* (near-analog recovery masquerading as generalization) is not new. What appears genuinely new is the specific *combination*: (a) explicitly operationalizing the bias as a trainable/measurable zero-shot NN-search model (rather than just a post-hoc audit or split-design fix), (b) applying a DebiasedDTA-style guide/predictor training-time correction to the *residual* this bias cannot explain, and (c) doing so specifically for drug *indication*/repurposing labels (ChEMBL indications across nine therapeutic classes) rather than binding affinity or graph-structural popularity.

5. **No paper was found** that (a) uses a chemical-similarity NN-search model as the DebiasedDTA-style "guide" and (b) targets indication/repurposing labels as the "predictor" task. This specific pairing appears to be the genuine, defensible gap — but the write-up should cite DRDM and TAPB explicitly as "related but distinct debiasing-for-repurposing/DTI work" rather than imply no one has thought about debiasing in this space, since that broader claim is false and would be an easy reviewer catch.

**Caveat on the RECOMB 2023 venue claim**: DebiasedDTA and pydebiaseddta were both published back-to-back (pages 1226-1239 and 1240-1245) in *Journal of Computational Biology* vol. 30, issue 11 (2023). RECOMB's own call-for-papers process for that year stated that accepted papers not published elsewhere would be offered a *J. Comput. Biol.* special issue, and the consecutive page numbers are consistent with a dedicated proceedings issue. However, I could not independently pull the RECOMB 2023 official accepted-papers list or an issue editorial explicitly labeling JCB 30(11) as "the RECOMB 2023 issue." The attribution "DebiasedDTA (accepted RECOMB 2023)" is very likely correct but is not 100% independently verified by this review — recommend a quick direct check of the RECOMB 2023 program (recomb.org proceedings page or the EasyChair RECOMB2023 archive) before stating this as fact in the paper's related-work section.

## 4. Recommended evaluation protocol / baseline list

Based on what is standard in this literature:

- **Splitting protocol**: Do not rely on a single scaffold split. The scaffold-split-overestimation literature (#5) and the similarity-aware-evaluation literature (#6) both show scaffold splits still leak train-test similarity. Recommend reporting results stratified by a continuous nearest-neighbor Tanimoto-similarity-to-training-set score (which the proposal already computes for its NN-search baseline), not just a binary "novel scaffold" bucket — this directly follows the similarity-aware-evaluation framework in #6 and preempts the most likely reviewer critique (that "novel scaffold" and "low similarity" are not synonymous, per #5).
- **Report both warm (near-analog) and cold (novel-scaffold) performance**, following DebiasedDTA's own warm/cold reporting convention (#1) — this makes the "most of the apparent accuracy comes from near-analogs" claim directly comparable to the DTA literature's own framing.
- **Baselines to include, confirmed standard and appropriately scoped**:
  - **Chemprop (D-MPNN)** (#12): legitimate, standard, actively maintained baseline for the "plain fingerprint/graph model." No adjustment needed for featurization, but note Chemprop was benchmarked on property-prediction tasks, not indication labels — the proposal should state it is repurposing Chemprop's architecture for a new (indication-classification) task, which is normal practice but worth a sentence of justification.
  - **MolCLR/GROVER-type pretrained encoder**: reasonable as a "does self-supervised chemical pretraining already capture the residual signal" control; no legitimacy concerns found, but this review did not independently verify MolCLR/GROVER citation counts (out of scope for the searches run; flagged as unconfirmed if the final paper needs a specific citation).
  - **TxGNN (#11)**: legitimate and prominent, but **needs adjustment** before use as a baseline. TxGNN is a graph-foundation model trained on PrimeKG and evaluated with a *disease-centric, graph zero-shot* protocol (holding out entire diseases from the knowledge graph), not a chemical novel-scaffold protocol. Using it "only where drugs overlap" (as planned) is the right instinct, but the paper should explicitly note that TxGNN's zero-shot notion (unseen disease in the KG) is not the same construct as this proposal's zero-shot notion (unseen chemical scaffold), so a head-to-head comparison measures something different in each model's favor and should be framed as a complementary reference point, not an apples-to-apples ablation.
  - **Target-knowledge-only model**: appropriately placed as an ablation to isolate the contribution of the text/target view independent of chemistry.
- **Leakage audits**: Follow the structure of the two confirmed clinical-ML leakage papers (#14, #15): (a) a quantitative audit showing performance with vs. without the leaking feature class (PubMed-abstract text about the drug), and (b) a term-masking robustness check (masking seizure/class-defining terms), mirroring the "same-admission ICD code" masking logic in #15. This gives the leakage section a citable methodological precedent rather than presenting it as an ad hoc check.
- **Statistical reporting**: Given the small therapeutic-class sample sizes implied by "nine therapeutic classes," report confidence intervals or bootstrap variance on AUROC differences (DebiasedDTA itself reports standard deviations across 5 reruns per setting, #1) rather than single point estimates, especially for the headline 0.73-vs-0.56-type comparisons.
